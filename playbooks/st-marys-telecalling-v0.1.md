# St. Mary's Telecalling Playbook v0.1

Date: 2026-09-18  
Applies to: admissions cold calls, warm enquiries, follow-ups, counselling/closing calls.

This is a process guide, not a word-for-word script. Agents should sound natural while following the principles.

## Non-negotiables

- Be truthful.
- Identify St. Mary's transparently.
- Respect time, privacy, no/opt-out requests and applicable calling rules.
- Ask before assuming.
- Listen before recommending.
- Use only approved/current facts.
- Never manufacture fear, false scarcity, fake familiarity or unsupported guarantees.
- Every meaningful call should produce either a clear next step or a clear reason the lead should not advance now.

---

## 0. Pre-call preparation

Before dialing, know what is available in the CRM:

- lead source;
- class/program of interest;
- previous enquiry/calls/messages;
- important notes already provided by the family;
- last outcome and promised follow-up;
- approved current information relevant to the programme;
- whether there is any recorded opt-out / do-not-call restriction.

Define the **one primary objective** for this call.

Examples:
- cold lead: establish relevance + qualify + earn next step;
- warm enquiry: understand criteria + answer/match + next step;
- follow-up: resolve the open issue + advance or close the loop;
- counselling/closing: support a fit decision with facts.

---

## 1. Opening

### Cold call

Use a transparent, concise opening.

Example:

> "Hello, is this Mr./Ms. [Name]? I'm [Agent] calling from St. Mary's regarding [truthful reason/source]. Is now a bad time for a brief conversation?"

Alternative when the lead source is known:

> "Hello, I'm [Agent] from St. Mary's. We received your interest regarding Grade 11/ISC [or actual programme]. I wanted to understand what you're looking for and see if we can help. Is this a convenient time?"

### Important

Do **not** use a deliberately vague "old friend" opener to make a cold prospect wonder whether they already know the caller.

Do not make the agent promise an artificial duration such as exactly 30 seconds unless that promise is genuine.

---

## 2. Relevance check

Before giving the full institutional pitch, establish whether there is a live need.

Examples:

- "Are you currently exploring Grade 11 options?"
- "Are you looking mainly at Science, Commerce, or still deciding?"
- "Is NEET/JEE integration something you're considering, or are you looking primarily at the board programme?"
- "What stage are you at right now — just collecting information, comparing colleges, or ready to visit/decide?"

If there is no present relevance, do not force the full pitch. Capture timing or respectfully close.

---

## 3. Discovery

The objective is to understand **goal, concern, criteria, timing and decision context** — not to interrogate the parent.

Choose only the questions that fit the conversation.

### Goal

- "What are you hoping the next two years should help your child achieve?"
- "What is the student's current goal — medicine, engineering, another path, or still exploring?"

### Decision criteria

- "What matters most to you when choosing the school/college?"
- "What are the main things you're comparing between institutions?"
- "Of academics, competitive-exam support, faculty, hostel/transport, fees, environment and location, which are most important to you?"

### Concern / current situation

- "What has been the main concern so far?"
- "When you say the workload is a concern, what part worries you most?"
- "Could you give me an example so I understand properly?"
- "How long has that been something you've been thinking about?" — only when relevant.

### Timing

- "When are you planning to make the decision?"
- "Are there any dates or exams you are waiting for before deciding?"

### Decision context

- "Who else should be part of the discussion before you finalise?"
- "Would the student also like to speak with the counsellor/faculty?"

Do not ask sensitive or deeply emotional questions simply to produce pain or urgency.

---

## 4. Reflect and confirm

Before recommending, demonstrate that you understood.

Example:

> "So if I've understood correctly, your top priorities are strong NEET preparation without compromising board performance, and you're also concerned about hostel supervision. Is that right?"

If the parent corrects you, update the picture before pitching.

---

## 5. Recommend — only what is relevant

Use the structure:

`Their need -> relevant St. Mary's fact -> why it matters -> next logical step`

Avoid an information dump.

Bad pattern:

> "We have X, Y, Z, hostel, sports, labs, results, integrated coaching..."

Better pattern:

> "Since you said NEET preparation and board balance are your two priorities, let me explain specifically how our integrated structure handles those two areas..."

Use no more proof than needed to answer the stated concern. Every result, rank, scholarship, seat claim, fee, deadline and policy must be current and approved.

---

## 6. Objection handling — ACAN

### A — Acknowledge

Show that you heard the objection without arguing.

- "I understand."
- "That's a fair concern."
- "Yes, I can see why you'd compare that."

### C — Clarify

Find the actual issue.

Examples:

- Fee: "When you say the fee feels high, is the main concern the total amount, payment timing, or whether the value is different from the alternatives you're comparing?"
- Distance: "Is the concern mainly daily travel time, safety, or that the student may get tired?"
- Need to discuss: "Of course. What parts do you think your spouse/student will want clarity on so I can make sure you have the right information?"
- 'Need to think': "Sure. What are the main things you still need to evaluate?"

### A — Answer

Answer the clarified issue directly and factually.

Do not counter every objection with another question. Do not hide fees or other factual information to maintain "control."

### N — Next step / check

- "Does that answer the concern?"
- "Would it help if I sent the fee structure and called you tomorrow after you've reviewed it?"
- "Would a campus visit with the counsellor make the comparison easier?"

If the answer remains no, respect it.

---

## 7. Close for the correct next step

A cold/warm admissions call often should not attempt to force the final admission. Close for the most appropriate next commitment.

Possible next steps:

- campus visit;
- counselling appointment;
- faculty/academic interaction;
- hostel visit;
- scholarship/eligibility check;
- brochure/fee sheet plus scheduled callback;
- application initiation;
- final confirmation/payment process when the family is genuinely ready;
- respectful closure/no further follow-up.

Whenever possible, make the next step specific:

> "Would Saturday at 11:00 AM or 3:00 PM work better for the campus visit?"

Do not manufacture urgency if no real deadline/scarcity exists.

---

## 8. CRM after-call discipline

Every meaningful connected call should record:

- call outcome;
- programme/stage;
- important parent/student goals;
- decision criteria;
- objections/root concerns;
- what information was provided;
- exact next action;
- owner;
- scheduled follow-up date/time if applicable;
- no-call/opt-out status when relevant.

The next caller should not make the family repeat everything unnecessarily.

# Call goals by funnel stage

## Cold
**Win:** attention + relevance + basic qualification + next step.  
**Not required:** full institutional presentation or final close.

## Warm enquiry
**Win:** understand needs + relevant answer/match + advance to visit/counselling/application.

## Follow-up
**Win:** reference the previous conversation, resolve the open item, and agree the next step or close the loop.

## Counselling / closing
**Win:** ensure fit, answer decision-critical questions, make the process clear, and support an informed decision.

---

# Prohibited patterns

Do not train or reward:

- fake familiarity;
- hiding caller identity;
- shaming a parent/student;
- fear-based statements about the child's future;
- repeated pressure after a clear no;
- unsupported competitor attacks;
- false deadlines/scarcity;
- unverified result/ranking/scholarship claims;
- guarantees of exam results/admission outcomes;
- treating affordability as something the agent must overpower;
- rapid-fire question interrogation;
- deliberately refusing direct factual questions to retain control.

---

# Manager coaching rule

Coach **one or two high-impact behaviours at a time**. Do not give an agent 15 corrections after one call.

A useful coaching sequence is:

1. play the exact moment;
2. ask the agent what they noticed;
3. identify the relevant principle ID;
4. rewrite/re-role-play the moment;
5. listen for the behaviour again in future calls;
6. track whether the downstream metric improves.
