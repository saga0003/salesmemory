# Source Audit — Initial YouTube Sales Material

Date: 2026-09-18  
Scope: Five transcripts supplied by the user for possible use in St. Mary's admissions telecalling.

The goal of this audit is not to rate the creators. It is to decide which ideas should enter the St. Mary's operating system, which should be tested, and which should stay out.

## Source 1 — Precision probing / NEPQ-style questioning

### Keep
- Ask for specific examples rather than accepting vague surface statements.
- Explore how long a concern has existed when genuinely relevant.
- Ask how the issue affects the student's/parent's decision.
- Reflect meaningful words or summarize them to invite clarification.
- Slow down important questions and allow the prospect time to think.
- Use an empathetic, concerned tone rather than firing questions mechanically.
- Avoid highly personal or emotional questions before sufficient rapport/relevance exists.

### Modify for St. Mary's
- Replace "build pain" with **understand the gap between current situation and desired outcome**.
- Replace "make them relive pain" with **understand the concern and its practical impact**.
- Future-pacing can be useful when factual and appropriate: e.g. "What would a good Grade 11 experience look like for your child?"

### Reject
- "Pain drives change" as a universal sales law.
- "Human beings buy 100% on emotion; brain studies prove it." This is an overclaim.
- Any deliberate attempt to intensify parent anxiety so they become easier to close.

**Decision:** useful discovery mechanics; unsuitable psychological framing in parts.

## Source 2 — 3A reframing / objection handling

### Keep
- Acknowledge what the prospect said before responding.
- Clarify ambiguous questions/objections rather than guessing.
- Avoid argumentative rebuttals.
- State facts and tell the truth.
- Do not sell an unqualified prospect simply to win a transaction.

### Modify for St. Mary's
The creator's `Acknowledge -> Associate -> Ask` can become a safer admissions version:

`Acknowledge -> Clarify -> Answer -> Next step/check (ACAN)`

Positive association is optional and must be genuine. Do not tell a parent that an objection makes them "like our best customers" simply as a persuasion device.

### Reject
- The idea that the question-asker should maintain control over the prospect.
- Avoiding legitimate questions merely to keep control of the conversation.
- The claim that inviting questions is inherently bad.
- Repeated closing attempts simply because top closers "ask more times."

**Decision:** strong objection-clarification core; remove adversarial/control framing.

## Source 3 — Cold calling, market research, objection handling and tonality

### Keep
- Cold outreach can be a useful channel when the audience and purpose are appropriate and the calling process is compliant.
- Define the target lead/customer segment before calling.
- For research, ask "why/what" questions rather than treating superficial yes/no answers as validation.
- Keep the opening concise and create relevance quickly.
- Ask whether it is a suitable time to continue.
- Use questions to uncover the real issue behind an objection.
- Acknowledge before educating/re-aligning.
- Train voice delivery: friendly/inviting, calm/reassuring, confident when presenting factual information, and empathetic when the concern warrants it.

### Test locally
- The creator's "30 seconds of grace" is not a universal scientific rule. Test opening length and wording on St. Mary's connected calls.
- The six-tonality taxonomy is a useful coaching model, not established St. Mary's evidence. Test whether it improves clarity, engagement and next-step conversion.

### Reject
- Fake "old friend" openings intended to make a cold prospect think the caller might already know them.
- "If they picked up, they have time."
- "The objective is to extract pain" or treating the caller as a therapist.
- Any blanket assumption that objections are merely barriers to be engineered away.

**Decision:** substantial tactical value after removing deception and pain-extraction framing.

## Source 4 — Communication skills / voice, structure and self-review

### Keep
- Flat/monotone delivery can reduce engagement.
- Excessive filler words and rambling reduce clarity.
- Record/transcribe practice calls and review them for clarity, concision, filler, repetition and structure.
- Use voice intentionally: pace, volume, pitch variation, emotion/warmth and pauses.
- Structured answers are easier to follow than thinking aloud in circles.

### Modify for St. Mary's
- Body-language coaching is secondary for audio-only calls. Posture may help voice delivery, but call scoring should focus on what can actually be heard.
- Do not reward theatrical vocal variation. Natural clarity and warmth are more important.

### Test locally
- Treat the exact "five dials" model as a coaching framework, not as a validated scientific scale.

**Decision:** highly useful for agent coaching and self-review; keep the phone-relevant parts.

## Source 5 — Cold calling/content podcast

### Keep
- A cold call's objective does not always need to be the final sale; earning the right next step can be a better goal.
- Curiosity and qualification before recommendation are useful.
- In "sell me this pen" style examples, understanding whether there is a need before pitching is the useful lesson.
- Value should be explained in terms of the prospect's actual needs rather than generic features.

### Reject
- "Only ask questions / never make statements."
- Fake-familiarity openings.
- Claims that enough perceived value means a prospect will somehow find, borrow or create budget.
- Financial or emotional pressure to make a family stretch beyond a stated affordability constraint.
- Treating short social-media cold-call clips as proof that a method handles the complete complexity of an admissions conversation.

**Decision:** retain curiosity, qualification and next-step orientation; reject the more extreme persuasion claims.

# What enters St. Mary's v0.1

1. Transparent identity and reason for calling.
2. Permission/time check when appropriate.
3. Short relevance check.
4. Targeted discovery around goals, concerns, criteria and timing.
5. Listening, reflection and summary.
6. Recommendation based only on what is relevant to that family.
7. Objection handling using ACAN.
8. Accurate proof and approved claims only.
9. Explicit next step when there is fit.
10. CRM follow-up discipline.
11. Respect for a clear no / later / opt-out.
12. Ongoing call review and controlled experimentation.

# What stays out

- Deliberately amplifying fear about a child's future.
- Deceptive familiarity or hidden identity.
- Unsupported guarantees, rankings, results, scarcity or scholarship claims.
- Adversarial "control" thinking.
- Repeated pressure after a clear decision.
- Treating affordability as an objection to overpower.
- Rigid talk ratios, exact question counts or arbitrary 30-second rules without St. Mary's data.

# Audit conclusion

The material contains useful sales mechanics, but it should **not** be copied as a doctrine. The strongest reusable ideas are listening, adaptive questioning, clarification, concise relevance, natural voice delivery, truthfulness, qualification, value-to-need mapping and explicit next steps. The weakest parts are creator-specific neuroscience claims, pain extraction, deceptive pattern interrupts, domination/control language and excessive closing pressure.
