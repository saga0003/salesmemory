# Validated Principles — St. Mary's Sales Memory

Version: 0.1  
Date: 2026-09-18

This is the canonical principle registry for future call reviews. A creator's technique is not automatically a St. Mary's standard. Each principle carries an evidence status and a St. Mary's interpretation.

## Evidence labels

- **A — Strong:** supported by peer-reviewed synthesis/meta-analysis or multiple strong sources.
- **B — Good / transferable:** good evidence or large observational conversation data, but not specifically St. Mary's admissions.
- **C — Hypothesis:** plausible technique or creator experience that must be tested on St. Mary's calls.
- **D — Reject / do not operationalize:** unsupported overclaim, deceptive/manipulative framing, or poor fit for education admissions.

---

## Canonical principles

### P001 — Listen to understand before recommending
**Status: A**

Do not jump from a parent's first statement directly into a pitch. Listen for the underlying goal, concern, comparison criteria, timing, and decision stage before recommending a program or next step.

Why it stays: salesperson listening is associated with adaptive selling and relationship/performance outcomes, and adaptive selling itself has substantial research support.

**St. Mary's use:** the agent should be able to state, in the parent's own terms, what matters before explaining why St. Mary's may be relevant.

### P002 — Adapt the conversation; do not read a rigid script
**Status: A**

Use a consistent process, not identical wording. The call should adapt to lead source, program, parent/student goals, objections, and stage in the admissions funnel.

**St. Mary's use:** scripts are guardrails and examples. An agent who mechanically reads every question in order is not demonstrating good selling.

### P003 — Use diagnostic questions, not a monologue or an interrogation
**Status: B**

Targeted questions help uncover fit and decision criteria. Large-scale B2B conversation analyses also suggest that winning conversations tend to be more balanced and that simply asking more questions is not necessarily better.

**St. Mary's use:** score the quality and relevance of questions, not a magic question count. Questions should be distributed naturally through the conversation.

### P004 — Reflect and summarize what the prospect means
**Status: B**

Brief reflection — including repeating a meaningful word, paraphrasing, or summarizing — can show that the agent is listening and can invite clarification.

**St. Mary's use:** prefer natural phrases such as: "So your main concern is the daily travel time, right?" or "When you say the workload worries you, what part are you most concerned about?"

Do not deliberately intensify anxiety or make a parent relive distress simply to create urgency.

### P005 — Clarify an objection before answering it
**Status: B**

An objection is often ambiguous. Acknowledge it, clarify what the person actually means, answer the real issue factually, and check whether it was resolved.

**St. Mary's use:**

`Acknowledge -> Clarify -> Answer -> Next step/check (ACAN)`

Example: "I understand. When you say the fee feels high, is the bigger concern the total cost, payment timing, or whether the value justifies it?"

Direct factual questions must still be answered. Clarification must never be used to evade questions about fees, results, scholarships, hostel, transport, curriculum, or policies.

### P006 — Use a concise, transparent opening and earn permission to continue
**Status: C (high-priority local test)**

The first moments of a cold call should quickly establish who is calling, why, and whether it is a reasonable time to continue.

**St. Mary's use:** identify St. Mary's promptly. Never create fake familiarity to make the parent wonder whether they already know the caller.

The exact opener, wording, and ideal length should be tested on St. Mary's calls rather than copied from a creator's "30-second" rule.

### P007 — Map the recommendation to the stated goal
**Status: A/B**

Do not list every feature of the institution. Select the one to three facts most relevant to what the parent/student said and explain the connection.

**St. Mary's use:**

`Need/goal -> relevant St. Mary's fact -> why it matters -> suitable next step`

Only use current, approved facts and claims.

### P008 — Tell the truth and qualify for fit
**Status: Required operating principle**

Do not force every lead toward admission. If a program is not a good fit, say so. Never invent rankings, results, scholarships, seat scarcity, deadlines, guarantees, or competitor claims.

**St. Mary's use:** good sales means helping the family reach a well-informed next decision, not manufacturing a yes.

### P009 — Tone, pacing and pauses are coaching dimensions
**Status: B/C**

Voice delivery affects clarity and perceived confidence. Useful dimensions include pace, volume, pitch variation, warmth, emphasis, and pauses.

**St. Mary's use:** coach agents away from rushing, flat delivery, excessive filler words, robotic scripts, or aggressive pressure. Do not force every agent into one artificial "sales voice."

### P010 — End qualified conversations with an explicit next step
**Status: B/C**

A productive admissions call should usually finish with a clear action rather than a vague "we'll talk later."

Examples: campus visit, counselling session, brochure/fee sheet followed by a scheduled callback, application step, scholarship eligibility check, or a respectful close when there is no fit.

### P011 — Follow-up is part of the sale, not an afterthought
**Status: Operational standard**

Every meaningful call should leave the CRM with the current status, important needs/objections, owner, next action, and next-action date/time when applicable.

A strong call with a missing follow-up can still become a lost lead.

### P012 — Preserve autonomy and respect
**Status: Required operating principle**

Parents and students must be allowed to ask questions, compare alternatives, say no, request a later call, or opt out. The agent's job is to guide the conversation, not dominate it.

Commercial calling and consent/opt-out processes must comply with applicable Indian telecom rules and the organisation's approved calling process.

## Rejected or modified creator claims

### R001 — "People buy 100% on emotion"
**Status: D — Reject**

Emotion can strongly influence decisions, but evidence does not support the claim that human choice is 100% emotional. Admissions decisions also involve cost, academics, location, logistics, eligibility, family preferences, and other deliberate considerations.

### R002 — "The person asking the questions is in control"
**Status: D — Reject as doctrine**

Replace this with **structured conversation leadership**. Questions are useful for understanding and guiding, but a parent should not feel controlled or cross-examined.

### R003 — Fake-familiarity / "old friend" opening
**Status: D — Reject**

Do not intentionally make a cold prospect wonder whether they already know the caller. St. Mary's should identify itself transparently.

### R004 — "Never make statements; only ask questions"
**Status: D — Reject**

Good admissions calls require truthful explanations, useful answers, and concise recommendations as well as questions.

### R005 — "Ask for the sale as many times as possible"
**Status: D — Reject for education admissions**

Persistence should mean responsible follow-up and resolving genuine uncertainty, not repeated pressure after a clear decision.

### R006 — "If they see enough value, they will find/borrow the budget"
**Status: D — Reject**

Do not pressure families into financial decisions. Discuss fees, aid/scholarship options, and payment structure accurately; respect affordability constraints.

### R007 — "Do you have any questions? is always a bad question"
**Status: D — Reject**

Families should be explicitly invited to ask questions. A stronger wording can be "What questions do you have before we decide the next step?" but the principle of allowing questions is essential.

### R008 — "If they answered the phone, they have time"
**Status: D — Reject**

Answering is not consent to a sales conversation. Check whether it is a suitable time and respect the answer.

### R009 — "Extract pain" / "act like a therapist"
**Status: D — Reject as St. Mary's language and objective**

Replace with **understand goals, concerns, consequences and decision criteria**. Admissions callers are not therapists and should not amplify fear about a child's future to create urgency.

### R010 — Universal talk/listen ratios or question counts
**Status: D as a hard rule; B as exploratory analytics**

Large sales datasets can offer useful directional signals, but ratios such as 43/57 or fixed question counts come from different sales contexts and should not become St. Mary's quotas without local evidence.

## Evidence base used for v0.1

- Franke, G.R. & Park, J.-E. (2006). *Salesperson Adaptive Selling Behavior and Customer Orientation: A Meta-Analysis*. Journal of Marketing Research. https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.43.4.693
- Itani, O.S., Goad, E.A. & Jaramillo, F. (2019). Meta-analytic work on salesperson listening, adaptive selling, and performance. Journal of Business Research. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2019.04.048
- Lerner, J.S. et al. (2015). *Emotion and Decision Making*. Annual Review of Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-010213-115043
- Gong conversation-data analyses — useful observational B2B evidence, not causal evidence and not a direct admissions benchmark: https://www.gong.io/blog/talk-to-listen-conversion-ratio/
- Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI), TCCCPR/UCC consumer-protection materials and amendments. Operational compliance should be checked against the current TRAI rules and St. Mary's telecom provider setup.

## Local validation rule

If external evidence is strong, use the principle while still measuring St. Mary's outcomes. If evidence is C-level, run a controlled local experiment before making it a standard. If evidence is D-level, do not train agents to use it.
